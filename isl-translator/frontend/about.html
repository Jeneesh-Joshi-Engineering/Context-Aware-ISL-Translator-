Why we built this

Access starts with a conversation.

At a railway counter or public service desk, asking for help should be straightforward. ISL Bridge connects Deaf and Hard-of-Hearing visitors with staff through two synchronized screens.

Visitors scan a permanent counter sign and use their camera to sign. Recognized words become English and Hindi sentences; staff can reply by voice or typing in either language.

Our current prototype

This academic demonstration recognizes 12 service words: Counter, Entrance, Exit, Help, Money, Police, Receipt, Security, Ticket, Train, When and Where, with a No_Gesture idle class. The earlier alphabet experiment is archived. It does not interpret arbitrary Indian Sign Language sentences. Hand tracking and recognition run locally; camera video is not sent to the server.

Voice input uses your browser’s speech service and may require internet. Online translation sends recognized text to the configured language service. Without it, supported counter phrases have local bilingual translations; other replies are clearly marked when translation is unavailable.
